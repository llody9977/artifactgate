[01] Risk Assessment

The Business Problem & Supply Chain Risk

Software supply chain vulnerabilities are business risks requiring explicit admission controls.

Risk 1

Unknown Origin

Is the upstream container image genuinely published by the vendor? Has the publisher registry account or build pipeline been compromised?

Risk 2

Mutable Tag Identity

Container tags (`:latest`, `:v2`) can change silently upstream. A tag scanned in staging may resolve to completely different bytes when deployed to production.

Risk 3

Unknown Composition

Images may contain unpatched CISA KEV vulnerabilities, embedded API credentials, trojanized malware binaries, or copyleft licensing liabilities (AGPL-3.0).

Risk 4

Inconsistent Risk Acceptance

Exceptions are stored informally in developer tickets without expiration dates, accountable risk owners, or standardized evaluation thresholds.

Risk 5

Post-Release Security Decay

Vulnerabilities and active exploits are discovered continuously after image promotion. An image approved last month may harbor critical zero-days today.

Risk 6

Deployment Bypass

CI/CD pipeline scans are useless if deployment tools do not re-verify decision attestations and digests before launching containers in production.

[02] Control Specification

Admission Control Requirements

What an enterprise container artifact admission system must deliver.

Functional Requirements

  • Bind all decisions to immutable SHA-256 digests & `linux/amd64` architecture.
  • Enforce upstream source image allowlists & version intake rules.
  • Collect static (CVE, secret, license, malware) & dynamic (eBPF) evidence.
  • Evaluate normalized evidence using versioned OPA Policy-as-Code.
  • Enforce machine-readable, time-bounded risk exceptions (OpenVEX).
  • Keylessly attest promotion decisions using OIDC identities via Cosign.
  • Verify attestation predicates at deployment time before container execution.
  • Re-scan promoted workloads weekly to challenge prior approval decisions.

Security & Risk Requirements

  • Fail-Closed Posture: Reject promotion if scan evidence is missing or malformed.
  • Quarantine Isolation: Execute dynamic observation in bounded sandboxes.
  • Least-Privilege OIDC: Restrict workflow permissions & keyless signing identities.
  • Separation of Duties: Isolate policy owners from workload requestors.
  • Immutable Audit Trails: Retain tamper-evident signed attestation records.
  • Tag Drift Prevention: Enforce digest-only promotion and deployment paths.

[04] System Architecture

4-Plane Admission Architecture

Decoupling evidence collection, policy decisioning, and deployment enforcement.

[ GOVERNANCE PLANE ] Policies · Exceptions · Approvals · Risk Owners │ ▼ UNTRUSTED ASSESSMENT TRUSTED SOURCE PLANE ARTIFACT Registry ──► Resolve ──► Evidence ──► OPA Decision ──► Promote + Attest digest store │ │ │ v v └────────► [REJECTED] Controlled Registry │ v Admission Control │ v Runtime │ v Reassess / Revoke

1. Intake Plane

Resolves upstream vendor references (`n8nio/n8n`) to immutable SHA-256 digests for `linux/amd64`. Verifies upstream allowlists before fetching container layers.

2. Assessment Plane

Collects static vulnerability scans (Trivy), secret scans, license classifications, SPDX 2.3 SBOMs, ClamAV malware scans, and Tracee eBPF dynamic syscall observations in quarantine.

3. Decision Plane (OPA)

Normalizes evidence into canonical `evidence.json`. OPA Rego modules evaluate findings against declarative rules and output structured decision predicates (`promotion-decision.json`).

4. Enforcement Plane

Promotes approved digest pairs to protected GHCR registry paths. Keylessly signs Cosign attestations. `install.sh` verifies signatures and predicates before container initialization.

[05] Policy Engine

Open Policy Agent (OPA) Policy-as-Code Engine

Scanners collect evidence. OPA decides acceptability.

10 Declarative Policy Domains

  • POL-SRC: Source allowlists (`n8nio/n8n`, `n8nio/runners`)
  • POL-ID: Platform architecture (`linux/amd64`) & digest binding
  • POL-EVD: Scanner completion flags & policy hash integrity
  • POL-VULN: CISA KEV catalog & EPSS exploit probability gating
  • POL-LIC: Prohibited copyleft & legal review license rules
  • POL-SEC: Secret detection & ClamAV malware blocking
  • POL-PROV: Upstream publisher signatures & SLSA attestations
  • POL-RUN: Tracee eBPF application tracing & runner exemptions
  • POL-EXC: OpenVEX waiver expiration & accepted CVE coverage
  • POL-DEC: Root aggregator producing standard outcome verdicts

7 Standardized Outcome Verdicts

APPROVED Zero blocking or review findings. Auto-promoted.
APPROVED_WITH_EXCEPTION Active, unexpired waiver covers CVEs.
MANUAL_REVIEW Review-required items exist (EPSS, runner exemption).
REJECTED Blocking policy violations exist (KEV, malware, AGPL).
EXPIRED Previously valid waiver has exceeded expiration.
REVOKED Previously approved artifact revoked during re-scan.
ERROR Missing evidence or malformed JSON input.

[08] Case Studies

Engineering Case Studies & Control Derivations

Real supply chain failures mapped to derived admission controls.

Case 1

`tj-actions/changed-files` (Action Compromise)

Failure Mode: Mutable release tags (`:v40`) were hijacked upstream to execute malicious secret exfiltration code.

ArtifactGate Control: All workflow actions and container intake resolve strictly to immutable SHA-256 commit digests.

Case 2

XZ Utils Backdoor (Targeted Logic Bomb)

Failure Mode: Trusted maintainer inserted build-time backdoor into tarball sources that bypassed static code review.

ArtifactGate Control: Tracee kernel eBPF dynamic syscall observation monitors unexpected binary execution in quarantine.

Case 3

SolarWinds (Vendor Build Compromise)

Failure Mode: Valid vendor code-signing certificates signed malicious build updates (`SUNBURST`).

ArtifactGate Control: Independently evaluates security evidence and attests downstream promotion predicates (`promotiondecision`).

Case 4

Equifax Apache Struts (Remediation SLA Breakdown)

Failure Mode: Known CVE-2017-5638 patch existed but unpatched application remained exposed for months.

ArtifactGate Control: OpenVEX waivers enforce accountable risk owners, expiration dates (`expires_on`), and active CVE coverage validation.
